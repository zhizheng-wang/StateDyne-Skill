# Search for better problem representations, not just more examples

One coherent proposal is a candidate, not closure. Independently reconstruct what user success actually means across distinct time horizons, protected values and feasibility constraints. Separate **present income from asset ownership, apparent progress from verifiable outcome, direct benefit from second-order option value**, or analogous domain-specific distinctions only when supported by task evidence. Do not assume any named example is required on every task.

Require a truly competing solution family when materially possible, not an altered label for the incumbent. Look for missing generative actors/conditions, external economic or evaluation environment, transferable relations from other domains, compound interventions and future states that reverse present ranking. Reconstruct the no-change and best existing alternative as real rivals. Distinguish users' stated goals from plausible replacements: model may propose more valuable ends but cannot decide private values without authorization.

A new path must identify a different potential outcome or causal transmission, a differentiating forecast or counterexample, likely opportunity/implementation cost, what evidence could reverse its ranking, and what can be explored without user participation. Do not substitute vague creativity, consultant clichés or long lists for a real improvement. If the incumbent survives strong alternatives, state the reason and stop. No predetermined number of ideas or fixed iterations; do not prematurely close because initial advice is plausible. Preserve important conditional inventions without falsely asserting they already exist.
